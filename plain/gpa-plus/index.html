GPA+ | Transferable Skills Benchmark

Long-Term Strategy: Helping students show employers the skills behind their GPA.

On this page: About | Why It Matters | Framework | Samples | Progress | Resources

About GPA+

GPA+, the Transferable Skills Benchmark, is the long-term strategy of Growing LSC-Tomball 3.0.

Goal Statement

To create a revolutionary measure that provides students with the ability to clearly demonstrate to potential employers the transferable skills they have learned, in addition to their grade point average.

The job-seeking landscape is changing, with more employers focusing on competencies such as critical thinking, collaboration, and communication. Lone Star College students will have a distinct advantage if they can present tangible evidence of the competencies they possess.

Why It Matters

  • The share of employers using GPA as a screening tool fell from 75% in 2019 to 46% in the latest report (NACE Job Outlook 2025).
  • 69% of US executives plan to prioritize hiring candidates with soft skills (LinkedIn).

Problems GPA+ Addresses

  1. GPA alone does not provide employers with evidence of the professional competencies they are seeking in college graduates.
  2. Employers are increasingly basing hiring decisions on professional competencies rather than GPA.
  3. LSC does not provide a tangible way for students to demonstrate to employers the professional competencies they have gained.
  4. Employers are developing their own mechanisms to gauge competencies.

Source: Growing LSC-Tomball 3.0 strategic plan (PDF), page 9.

Beyond the GPA: Bridging the Gap in Workforce Development

View the infographic

For the research behind these figures, read GPA Is Not Enough: The Workforce Need We Don’t Measure, an executive briefing by Kenneth Odom, PhD, chair of the Greater Tomball Area Chamber of Commerce’s Business and Education Partnership (PDF, 13 pages).

Text version of the infographic: Traditional credentials like GPAs and degrees only measure technical knowledge and training. In the modern economy, employers are shifting focus toward “formation”: the development of durable, human-centric skills that AI cannot easily replicate and that currently lack standardized measurement.

The Three Pillars of Workforce Preparation

  • Education and Training: mastery of technical knowledge and task-specific operational capabilities.
  • Formation, the Missing Pillar: enduring capacities like resilience and leadership that transfer across all contexts.
  • The Synergy Factor: training and education are less effective when isolated from formative capacity.

The Evidence: Why Durable Skills Dominate

  • The 80/20 interview split: employers spend 80% of interviews evaluating durable skills and 20% on technical skills.
  • Four times higher demand: employers seek durable skills four times more frequently than technical skills.
  • The AI differentiator: human judgment and collaboration are essential to bridge AI’s symbolic, abstract limitations.

Durable Skill Market Demand

  • Durable skills: 7 of the top 10 most requested job skills.
  • Texas job postings: 64% explicitly request at least one durable skill.
  • High-endurance jobs: highest demand in roles least likely to be automated.

The Measurement Frontier

  • The GPA+ initiative: mapping student learning outcomes to durable skill frameworks for employer reporting.
  • Performance-based micro-credentials: using specific assessments, such as presentations, to verify proficiency in empathy, initiative, and fluency.
  • Meaningful proxies: using specialized tools to measure “unmeasurable” traits like trust and leadership.

College 3.0 Framework

College 1.0: Course Grade

A letter or number indicating how well a student performed.

College 2.0: Overall Grade Point Average

A well-known and accepted tangible measurement indicating students' academic achievement.

College 3.0 (where we're going): GPA+ (Transferable Skills Benchmark)

A new measurement to supplement the GPA and document students' achievement of professional competencies that are transferable across roles and desired by employers.

Source: Growing LSC-Tomball 3.0 strategic plan (PDF), page 9.

Sample Reports
