The LSC-Tomball Commitment

Growing LSC‑Tomball 3.0

Introduced in Spring 2025, LSC-Tomball 3.0 encompasses a bold vision and strategic plan with objectives and goals to reach that future. Three primary strategies — short-term, mid-term, and long-term — are the basis of the plan.

Our vision

A national design challenge, a local plan

To accomplish our goals, LSC-Tomball joined the Reimagining Community Colleges Design Challenge (opens in a new tab), a national initiative led by Education Design Lab (the Lab).

The challenge supports institutions in crafting their strategic transformation plans using the Lab's human-centered design approach. The project focuses on advancing learner-centered, skills-based education to align with the future of work and fill regional skills gaps needed to fuel economic prosperity.

Three strategies

The plan, from near-term to long-term

Each strategy moves from a familiar starting point (1.0) to a new model of student success (3.0).

1 Short-term

249 Corridor of Opportunity Team 249

To harness the geographic proximity of LSC-University Park, LSC-Tomball, and the LSC-Magnolia Center, all located along SH-249, and collectively impact access, success, and post-graduation success of students along the corridor.

College 3.0 framework
  1. 1.0 Expand access: exchange the LSC-Creekside and LSC-Magnolia Centers with LSC-Montgomery
  2. 2.0 Collaborate for success: collaborate with LSC-University Park to anchor and create the 249 Corridor of Opportunity
  3. 3.0 Engage 249 corridor employers for improved opportunities for students
Updates & team: 249 Corridor of Opportunity

2 Mid-term

Community College Center for Neurodiversity Success

To create the Community College Center for Neurodiversity Success (CCCNS), a 501(c)3 non-profit organization dedicated to dramatically increasing access to higher education, improving completion rates, and enhancing post-graduation outcomes for neurodiverse individuals.

College 3.0 framework
  1. 1.0 Expand access for the neurodiverse population across the United States; more community colleges offer lifePATH®
  2. 2.0 Replicate lifePATH® and the OLSA degree; more neurodiverse students graduate with a credential
  3. 3.0 Increase meaningful employment opportunities for the neurodiverse population
Details & team: Center for Neurodiversity Success

3 Long-term

Transferable Skills Benchmark GPA+

To create a revolutionary measure that provides students with the ability to clearly demonstrate to potential employers the transferable skills they have learned, in addition to their grade point average.

College 3.0 framework
  1. 1.0 Course grade: a letter or number indicating how well a student performed
  2. 2.0 Overall grade point average: a well-known measure of academic achievement
  3. 3.0 GPA+: a new measurement that supplements the GPA and documents students' professional competencies
Details & team: GPA+
